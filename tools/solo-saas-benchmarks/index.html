SaaS benchmarks for solo founders

Published benchmarks come from companies far larger than yours. This page separates sourced figures from our own rules of thumb, and says where no data exists.

Published SaaS benchmarks are drawn from companies far larger than yours. Applying them to a one-person product produces one of two wrong conclusions: that you are failing when you are normal, or that you are fine when you are not.

Rough bands for a solo SaaS
  • Monthly churn: under 3% is strong and 3–7% is common for early-stage self-serve. Our rule of thumb, not a published benchmark.
  • Trial-to-paid, no card: 4–6% is good and 10–15% is great (ChartMogul, 2026).
  • Trial-to-paid, card required: 25–35% is good and 50–60% is great (ChartMogul, 2026).
  • Involuntary churn: 22% of all churn for SaaS on average (Churnkey, Stripe data, 2025). The cheapest churn to fix.
  • Time to $1k MRR: no reliable benchmark. Existing audience is the biggest variable.

Compare against your own last quarter before comparing against any of these.

Why standard benchmarks do not fit you

A widely cited 2026 conversion study was run by ChartMogul with ProductLed and Kyle Poyar. It covers 200 B2B software products, and a typical respondent sits between $1M and $10M ARR.

A solo founder at $4k MRR has about $48k ARR, 20 to 200 times smaller than that sample. The benchmark is not wrong. It measures a different kind of business, usually with more staff and enough customers for an average to mean something.

Two structural differences matter more than the size gap itself.

Your sample is too small for an average. With 20 customers, one cancellation moves your churn rate by five percentage points. The number on your dashboard is mostly noise, and reacting to it is how founders end up rebuilding things that worked.

Your price is probably lower. Lower-priced plans may attract more casual buyers, so segment your churn by plan before you compare it to anything.

Churn benchmarks

No public dataset covers one-person SaaS products reliably. The bands below are our own rule of thumb for early-stage self-serve products, not a published benchmark.

Under 3%33+ monthsStrong. Do not optimise further yet.
3–5%20–33 monthsCommon for self-serve.
5–7%14–20 monthsCommon early. Watch the trend.
Over 7%Under 14 monthsHard to grow: you replace more than 7% of customers every month before you add any.

Average lifetime is 1 ÷ monthly churn.

One arithmetic correction worth making, because it is the most common error founders make with this metric: annual churn is not monthly churn times twelve. It compounds, so 5% monthly is roughly 46% of customers lost over a year, not 60%.

Involuntary churn — the cheapest fix here

Failed and expired card payments cause involuntary churn. Churnkey puts it at an average of 22% of all churn for SaaS companies, using Stripe subscription data (November 2025). Your share depends on your customers and billing setup.

These customers did not decide to cancel. Their card failed. Retry logic, an expiry warning and a dunning sequence are a small project, and most solo founders have never checked their failed payment rate.

Check it before you act on anything else on this page. The churn reduction framework covers how it differs from the four other churn types.

Trial and conversion benchmarks

In the ChartMogul 2026 study, the median free-to-paid conversion was 8% across 200 products. Trials requiring a card converted at around 30%, more than five times the rate of trials without one.

The spread matters more than the median. The gap between the top and bottom fifth of products is roughly tenfold, so 8% is a poor target. Card-required trials capture fewer signups, so compare paying customers per 1,000 visitors, not conversion rate alone.

Where no reliable benchmark exists

Three numbers founders ask about that this page will not give, because the honest answer is that nobody has credible data at solo scale.

  • Time to first customer. Varies from a day to a year depending entirely on existing audience.
  • Landing page conversion for a one-person product. Published rates are dominated by funded companies with paid traffic.
  • "Normal" MRR growth rate. Solo growth is lumpy. Monthly percentages are close to meaningless below $5k.

For the one figure founders most want, how long to $1k MRR, no reliable benchmark exists. $1,000 MRR is 20 customers at $50 a month or 10 at $100. The $0 to $1k playbook covers what drives the count.

Use these bands to decide whether something is worth investigating, never to judge whether you are succeeding. At solo scale the trend in your own numbers carries far more information than your position against an industry median.

How to use these numbers

Three rules that keep benchmarks useful rather than demoralising.

Compare to yourself first. Your number last quarter versus this quarter is the only comparison with a matched sample.

Only act on a band you are clearly outside. At 4% churn against a 3–7% band, there is nothing to act on. At 11%, there is.

Segment before you conclude. If you have a $19 plan and a $99 plan, blending their churn produces a number describing neither. Tracking four numbers well beats tracking twelve badly — the four metrics that matter under $10k MRR covers which four.

Solo SaaS benchmarks FAQ

What is a good churn rate for a solo SaaS founder?

As a rule of thumb (ours, not a published benchmark), under 3% monthly churn is strong and 3–7% is common for early-stage self-serve products. With 20 customers a single cancellation moves your rate by five percentage points, so compare it with your own last three months first.

What is a good trial-to-paid conversion rate?

In the 2026 ChartMogul, ProductLed and Kyle Poyar report, a good rate for a trial without a card is 4–6% and a great rate is 10–15%. With a card required, good is 25–35% and great is 50–60%. The median across all 200 products was 8%.

Why do standard SaaS benchmarks not apply to solo founders?

Most published benchmarks come from companies between $1M and $10M ARR with support and onboarding teams. A solo product at $4k MRR is 20 to 200 times smaller than that sample, and with 20 customers a single cancellation moves your churn rate by five percentage points.

How much of SaaS churn is involuntary?

Churnkey puts involuntary churn at an average of 22% of all churn for SaaS companies, using Stripe subscription data (November 2025). Your share depends on your customers and billing setup, so count your own failed-payment cancellations over the last 90 days. It is the cheapest churn to fix: retry logic, expiry warnings and a dunning sequence are a small project.

How do I calculate annual churn from monthly churn?

Churn compounds, so it is not monthly times twelve. Use 1 minus (1 minus the monthly rate) to the twelfth power. At 5% monthly you lose roughly 46% of customers over a year rather than 60%.

How long does it take a solo founder to reach $1k MRR?

There is no reliable benchmark. No credible dataset covers solo founders at that stage, and existing audience is the biggest variable. Work from your own numbers: $1,000 MRR is 20 customers at $50 a month or 10 at $100.
