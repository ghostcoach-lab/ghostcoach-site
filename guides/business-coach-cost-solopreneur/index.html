How much does a business coach cost? The real 2026 numbers

Hourly rates, monthly retainers and package pricing — what business coaches actually charge in 2026, why the averages mislead solo founders, and the far cheaper alternative that covers the same weekly decisions.

Business coach cost sits between $150 and $500 per hour for most coaches, or $500 to $3,000 per month on retainer. Coaches who specialise in small businesses and solo operators sit lower, around $100 to $400 per hour. The spread is wide because "business coach" covers everything from a $20 book to a $60,000 twelve-month engagement.

The short answer
  • Hourly: $100 – $300 (newer coaches) · $200 – $600 (experienced) · $1,000+ (C-suite specialists)
  • Monthly retainer: $500 – $1,500 (small business) · $2,000 – $15,000 (growth and mid-market)
  • Packages: $10,000 – $60,000 for a six to twelve month engagement
  • Group programs: $200 – $1,000 per month per participant
  • AI coaching tools: $20 – $150 per month, no scheduling

For a solo founder under $30k MRR, the relevant question is not the headline price. It is the cost per decision — and on that measure the tiers are separated by orders of magnitude.

The same coach who is worth $2,000 a month at $50k MRR is rarely worth anything at $0 MRR. This guide breaks down what each tier charges, what it actually delivers, and which one matches your stage. It is written for solopreneurs running subscription software rather than for funded teams.

What business coaches charge, by pricing model

Business coaching fees come in four structures. The same coach may quote you three of them, and the quotes are harder to compare than they look.

Hourly. Maximum flexibility, minimum commitment, and the model most founders start with. The weakness is that every session spends its first fifteen minutes rebuilding context, so hourly coaching rarely compounds. Expect $100 to $300 from newer coaches and $200 to $600 from experienced ones.

Monthly retainer. The most common structure for ongoing work, usually two to four calls a month plus some email access. Small-business retainers run $500 to $1,500; growth-stage and mid-market run $2,000 to $15,000. This is the model that produces the best results, because continuity is where coaching value actually lives.

Package. A fixed engagement — commonly three, six or twelve months — bundled at a discount to the hourly rate and usually paid up front. Full programs run $10,000 to $60,000 depending on seniority and scope. The prepayment is the point: it buys the coach predictable revenue and buys you a commitment you cannot quietly abandon in month two.

Group and cohort. $150 to $600 per participant per session, or $200 to $1,000 a month. You trade personalisation for peers and a curriculum. Marketplaces like MentorCruise sit between this and 1:1 pricing — the GhostCoach vs MentorCruise comparison breaks down what that particular model includes.

The average cost of a business coach — and why the average misleads

The International Coaching Federation reports an industry average of roughly $272 per hour. That number gets quoted everywhere, and it is close to useless as a planning figure for a solo founder.

It blends a bootstrapped founder paying $150 an hour with a corporation paying $1,000 an hour for executive development. Averaging those two produces a number that describes neither. The band you should actually budget against, as a solo operator running subscription software, is $100 to $400 per hour or $500 to $1,500 a month.

A second rule of thumb circulates: budget two to three percent of annual revenue for coaching. That works for an established business with $500k of revenue. At $4k MRR it prescribes about $100 a month, which buys you no human coach at all — and that gap is exactly where the AI tier exists.

What you are actually paying for at each tier

Free options (books, content, communities)

Books give you frameworks at the cost of your time to read and apply them. YouTube and podcasts give you frameworks plus narrative context. Communities give you peer feedback and occasional insight.

What you do not get: accountability, structured application, or anyone with skin in your specific business doing well. This tier is genuinely useful at the very early stages, when you do not yet know what you do not know. Our guide to free coaching options covers how far you can get without paying anyone.

AI coaching tools ($20 to $150/month)

An AI tool gives you structured guidance, on-demand, applied to your specific situation. Good ones (the differentiated ones, not the generic chatbot wrappers) have a framework and produce specific recommendations, not balanced overviews. What you do not get: a human relationship, a network, accountability beyond what you self-impose. This tier is the sweet spot for solo founders at $0 to $30k MRR who need consistent direction without the cost of a human coach.

Group programs ($200 to $1,000/month)

You get a structured curriculum, a cohort of peers at similar stages, and group calls with the program leader. The group dynamic is the differentiator versus an AI tool — peer accountability and specific stories from people at your stage. You do not get truly personalized guidance; the program runs on a fixed cadence whether your week needs that topic or not. Best for founders who already know what they need to learn and want structure plus community.

1:1 human coaches ($500 to $5,000/month)

You get personalized attention, deep accountability, and a relationship with someone whose career outcome partly depends on your outcome. The best human coaches add value at every stage. The mediocre ones add value only at the stage of life they happen to be best at — usually the stage just past their own peak. Vet carefully. The AI vs human coach comparison covers when each is genuinely the right choice.

Executive coaching ($3,000+/month)

This tier is calibrated for funded founders and C-suite executives. The work is often less about tactics and more about psychology, decision-making frameworks, and structured leadership development. For a solo SaaS founder under $50k MRR, the price tag rarely produces returns proportional to the cost — the operational tactical needs of an early-stage business do not match what executive coaching is built for.

The hidden costs that the headline price misses

The headline price is rarely the full cost. Four hidden costs to factor in:

Time cost. A 1-hour weekly coaching session is 4 hours per month of synchronous time. Plus prep time, plus follow-through. For a solo founder, that is roughly 8 to 10 hours per month — time which itself has an opportunity cost. AI coaching has near-zero time overhead because it fits between other work.

Fit cost. A coach who is a bad fit costs you more than money. They give you advice you cannot use, which makes you doubt your own instincts. A poor fit is worse than no coach because it actively misleads. The cost of a fit failure includes the months you spent following advice that did not work, not just the fees you paid.

Scheduling cost. Coaches operate on calendars. You operate on your business's calendar. The mismatch produces missed sessions, rushed conversations, and topics covered out of order from when you actually needed them. AI coaches are available when the question arises, not on a schedule.

Switching cost. When a coach does not work out, leaving feels expensive — emotionally and financially. Most founders stay with a poor-fit coach 3 to 6 months longer than they should. Factor this into the price: a $1,500/month coach you stick with too long is closer to $10,000 in real cost.

Which option fits which stage

Spend should track the rate of decisions you are making, not the size of the ambition. A pre-revenue founder makes fewer consequential calls per month than one at $20k MRR fighting churn, and should spend accordingly. Our guide to coaching for bootstrapped founders goes deeper on the constraint. The honest matching, by stage:

  • Pre-launch, no revenue: Free options (books, communities) plus an AI tool. You do not yet know what you do not know. A human coach at this stage is premature optimization.
  • $0 to $10k MRR: AI coaching tool. Structured guidance, applied to your specific situation, at a fraction of human coach cost. Use the free options as supplementary reading.
  • $10k to $30k MRR: AI coaching plus a peer mastermind or cohort program. You now have data and decisions worth thinking through with peers at your stage. Human 1:1 coaching is borderline justifiable but rarely necessary at this stage.
  • $30k to $100k MRR: Consider a human coach for specific high-stakes decisions (hiring, fundraising, pivots). AI coaching continues to handle day-to-day strategy and execution questions.
  • $100k+ MRR: Full 1:1 human coaching plus AI for high-frequency questions. At this stage you can afford both and the marginal return on a human coach is high.

An honest assessment of AI coaching (including the limitations)

AI coaching is not a complete replacement for human coaching. It cannot give you the network access a well-connected human coach brings. It cannot read the room in a high-stakes conversation. It cannot hold a hard truth across multiple sessions with the persistence a human can. These are real limitations worth naming.

What AI coaching does well: applies a framework consistently to your specific situation, produces recommendations rather than menus of options, runs at the cost of a meal rather than a mortgage, is available the moment a question arises. For the operational and strategic decisions a solo founder makes weekly, these strengths usually outweigh the limitations. For the once-a-year high-stakes decisions (whether to raise, whether to sell, whether to pivot entirely), a human coach often adds value AI cannot.

There is also a return test worth running before you spend anything. If a coach helps you move from $29 to $59 on a hundred customers, that single decision pays for years of coaching at any tier — which is why pricing strategy is usually the highest-return topic to bring to a first session.

The right framing is "and" not "or" — AI for high-frequency low-to-medium-stakes decisions, human for low-frequency high-stakes ones. The wrong framing is "either or," which produces founders who either over-pay for human coaching they only use occasionally, or refuse to ever talk to a human and miss the moments where one matters.

The recommendation by MRR stage

If you are deciding this week rather than this year, run the number three ways before you commit to anything:

  1. Count your decisions. Write down the consequential calls you made last month — price changes, churn responses, channel bets. Under four, you need frameworks, not a coach. Over ten, you need frequency, and frequency is what human retainers cannot supply cheaply.
  2. Divide the fee by that count. A $1,500 retainer against six decisions is $250 a decision. A $79 tool against twenty is $4. Judge every quote on cost per decision, never on the monthly headline.
  3. Name what you are actually buying. Direction, accountability or access. AI covers direction well and accountability poorly. Only a human sells access, and it is the one thing worth paying $2,000 a month for. The SaaS coach guide covers how to vet for it.

Business coach cost FAQ

How much does a business coach cost?

Most business coaches charge $150 to $500 per hour, or $500 to $3,000 per month on retainer. Small-business specialists sit at the lower end, around $100 to $400 per hour. Six to twelve month packages commonly run $10,000 to $60,000 in total. AI coaching tools cover the same weekly decisions for $20 to $150 per month.

What do business coaches charge per hour?

Newer coaches typically charge $100 to $300 per hour. Experienced business coaches charge $200 to $600 per hour. C-suite and executive specialists charge $1,000 or more. Rates depend far more on track record and client size than on any certification.

What is the average cost of a business coach?

The International Coaching Federation reports an industry average of roughly $272 per hour. That average blends solo founders paying $150 an hour with corporations paying $1,000 an hour, so it is a poor planning number. Budget against the $100 to $400 band instead.

How much does business coaching cost per month?

Monthly retainers for small business owners commonly run $500 to $1,500. Growth-stage and mid-market retainers run $2,000 to $15,000. Group programs run $200 to $1,000 per month. AI coaching tools run $20 to $150 per month with no scheduling requirement.

Is an AI business coach a real alternative to a human coach?

For the weekly operational decisions a solo founder makes — pricing, churn, trial conversion, acquisition — yes. For once-a-year decisions like raising, selling or a full pivot, a human coach with a network adds something AI cannot. The honest framing is AI for high-frequency decisions, a human for low-frequency high-stakes ones.
