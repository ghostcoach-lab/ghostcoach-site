Running a product business alone means making decisions nobody checks. There is no co-founder to disagree with you, no team to notice when a plan quietly changes, and no one whose job it is to ask why you have avoided the same task for three weeks.

A solopreneur coach fills that gap. The problem with the traditional version is that it costs $500 to $2,000 a month and runs on someone else's calendar — which is exactly the wrong shape for a business that does not yet pay you a salary.

What a solopreneur coach does differently

Coaching for solopreneurs is not scaled-down executive coaching. The constraints are different enough that generic business advice actively misfires.

You have no team, so delegation advice is noise. Most business coaching assumes you can hand work to someone. The solo version has to be about what to remove, not who to give it to.

Your data is too small for standard frameworks. With forty customers, a churn rate swings several points on one cancellation. Advice built on benchmarks from companies with thousands of users produces confident conclusions from noise.

Your constraint is usually attention, not knowledge. Most solo founders already know roughly what they should do. What they lack is someone to decide which of four reasonable options to run this month.

Solopreneur coach FAQ

What is a solopreneur coach?

A solopreneur coach helps someone running a business alone make the decisions a co-founder or team would normally pressure-test. The difference from executive coaching is that there is no team to delegate to, so the advice has to be about what to remove rather than who to hand it to.

How much does solopreneur coaching cost?

Human coaching for solo founders typically costs $500 to $2,000 a month. AI coaching generally runs $20 to $150. GhostCoach starts at $79 a month with a 14-day free trial, with a $99 Operator tier and a one-time $499 Lifetime option capped at 50 places.

Is a coach for solopreneurs worth it?

It is worth it when your constraint is deciding rather than knowing — which is the common case for solo founders, who rarely lack information and usually lack anyone to disagree with them. It is poor value if what you need is accountability from a person or introductions.

How is this different from asking ChatGPT?

General assistants reason well but reset between sessions and give a balanced overview every time. A solopreneur business coach holds your product, stage, bottleneck and goal as structured context and commits to one recommendation, which is the part a stuck founder actually needs.

Who is GhostCoach not right for?

Agencies, service businesses, e-commerce and anyone who needs introductions or accountability from a real person. It is built specifically for solopreneurs running subscription software, from pre-launch to roughly $30k MRR.

Do I need a product before coaching helps?

No, and pre-launch is one of the strongest fits, because pricing and positioning mistakes made at launch are the most expensive and least reversible. Below roughly $1k MRR, though, customer conversations matter more than any coaching tool.
